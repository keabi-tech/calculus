FOUNDATION · 01

Introduction

Before studying differentiation and integration, we need to understand what mathematical functions represent.

Definition · Function

A function is a mathematical relationship that assigns exactly one output to each permitted input.

y = x²

This equation tells us that the output value, represented by y, is the square of the input value, represented by x.

Input xCalculationOutput y
11 × 11
22 × 24
33 × 39
44 × 416
Important idea

Differentiation studies how the output of a function changes as its input changes. Integration helps us recover accumulated quantities from their rates of change.

FOUNDATION · 02

Algebra foundations

Differentiation and integration use algebraic expressions. Understanding powers, coefficients, constants, and terms makes the rules easier.

1. Terms

5x³ + 4x² + 7x + 9

The expression contains four terms:

  • 5x³: coefficient 5, variable x, power 3.
  • 4x²: coefficient 4, variable x, power 2.
  • 7x: coefficient 7, variable x, power 1.
  • 9: constant term.

2. Powers

x³ = x × x × x

A power tells us how many times a variable is multiplied by itself.

3. Important algebra rules

Multiplication

x² × x³ = x⁵

Add the powers when multiplying the same base.

Division

x⁵ ÷ x² = x³

Subtract the powers when dividing the same base.

Power of a power

(x²)³ = x⁶

Multiply the powers.

Zero power

x⁰ = 1

For nonzero x, x⁰ equals one.

PART I · DIFFERENTIATION · 01

Meaning of a derivative

Definition

A derivative measures the instantaneous rate at which a function changes with respect to its input variable.

Consider a moving object. Its position changes as time passes. The derivative of its position describes its instantaneous velocity.

In geometry, a derivative represents the slope of a tangent line to a curve at a particular point.

Visual example: Slope of a curve

A tangent line touches the curve at the selected point. Its slope represents the derivative there.

Derivative notation

y = x²
dy/dx

The notation dy/dx means the derivative of y with respect to x.

Other common notations include:

Leibniz notation

dy/dx

Prime notation

y′
DIFFERENTIATION · 02

Constant rule

Definition

The derivative of a constant is zero because a constant does not change.

d/dx (c) = 0

Here, c represents any constant number.

Example 1 CONSTANT RULE
Question:
y = 15
1
Identify the expression.

The expression contains only the constant 15.

2
Apply the constant rule.

The derivative of every constant is zero.

dy/dx = 0
FINAL ANSWER 0
DIFFERENTIATION · 03

Power rule

Definition

The power rule allows us to differentiate an expression containing a variable raised to a power.

d/dx (xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹ Multiply by the power, then subtract one from the power.

Understanding the rule

Consider:

y = x⁴

There are two operations:

  1. Move the power 4 to the front as a multiplier.
  2. Subtract 1 from the original power.
4x⁴⁻¹ = 4x³
Example 2 POWER RULE
Question:
y = 7x⁵
1
Identify the coefficient and power.

Coefficient = 7. Power = 5.

2
Multiply the coefficient by the power.
7 × 5 = 35
3
Reduce the power by one.
5 − 1 = 4
4
Combine the results.
35x⁴
FINAL ANSWER dy/dx = 35x⁴

More examples

Example A

y = x²
y′ = 2x

Example B

y = 5x³
y′ = 15x²

Example C

y = 8x
y′ = 8

Example D

y = 9x⁶
y′ = 54x⁵
DIFFERENTIATION · 04

Sum and difference rules

Definition

We can differentiate each term separately when an expression contains addition or subtraction.

Sum rule

(u + v)′ = u′ + v′

Difference rule

(u − v)′ = u′ − v′
Example 3 MULTIPLE TERMS
Question:
y = 4x³ + 3x² + 5x + 8
1
Separate the terms.

We have four terms to differentiate individually.

2
Differentiate the first term.
4 × 3 × x² = 12x²
3
Differentiate the second term.
3 × 2 × x = 6x
4
Differentiate the third term.
5 × 1 = 5
5
Differentiate the constant.
8 becomes 0
FINAL ANSWER y′ = 12x² + 6x + 5
DIFFERENTIATION · 05

Constant multiple rule

Definition

When a function is multiplied by a constant, keep the constant and differentiate the function.

d/dx [c × u] = c × u′
Example 4 CONSTANT MULTIPLE
Question:
y = 6x⁴
1
Keep the constant 6.

We differentiate the power of x.

2
Apply the power rule.
6 × 4x³
3
Multiply.
24x³
FINAL ANSWER y′ = 24x³
DIFFERENTIATION · 06

Product rule

Definition

The product rule is used when two functions are multiplied together.

(uv)′ = u′v + uv′

In words:

  1. Differentiate the first function and multiply it by the second.
  2. Add the first function multiplied by the derivative of the second.
Example 5 PRODUCT RULE
Question:
y = x²(x³ + 2)
1
Identify the two functions.
u = x²
v = x³ + 2
2
Differentiate each function.
u′ = 2x
v′ = 3x²
3
Apply the product rule.
y′ = u′v + uv′
4
Substitute the expressions.
y′ = 2x(x³ + 2) + x²(3x²)
5
Expand and simplify.
= 2x⁴ + 4x + 3x⁴
= 5x⁴ + 4x
FINAL ANSWER y′ = 5x⁴ + 4x
DIFFERENTIATION · 07

Quotient rule

Definition

The quotient rule is used when one function is divided by another function.

(u/v)′ = (vu′ − uv′) / v²

The denominator v must not be zero.

Example 6 QUOTIENT RULE
Question:
y = (x² + 1) / x
1
Identify the numerator and denominator.
u = x² + 1
v = x
2
Differentiate both.
u′ = 2x
v′ = 1
3
Apply the quotient rule.
y′ = (v·u′ − u·v′) / v²
4
Substitute.
y′ = [x(2x) − (x² + 1)(1)] / x²
5
Simplify.
= (2x² − x² − 1) / x²
= (x² − 1) / x²
FINAL ANSWER y′ = (x² − 1) / x²
DIFFERENTIATION · 08

Chain rule

Definition

The chain rule is used when one function is contained inside another function.

dy/dx = (dy/du) × (du/dx)

We first differentiate the outside expression, then multiply by the derivative of the inside expression.

The chain rule follows the relationship between the inside and outside expressions.
Example 7 CHAIN RULE
Question:
y = (3x + 2)²
1
Identify the inside expression.
u = 3x + 2
2
Differentiate the outside expression.

The outside expression is u².

2u
3
Differentiate the inside expression.
du/dx = 3
4
Multiply the two derivatives.
2(3x + 2) × 3
5
Simplify.
6(3x + 2)
18x + 12
FINAL ANSWER y′ = 18x + 12
DIFFERENTIATION · 09

Higher-order derivatives

Definition

A higher-order derivative is obtained by differentiating an expression more than once.

The first derivative describes the rate of change of a function. The second derivative describes how that rate of change itself changes.

First derivative: y′
Second derivative: y′′
Example 8 SECOND DERIVATIVE
Question:
y = 4x³ + 2x² + 5x
1
Find the first derivative.
y′ = 12x² + 4x + 5
2
Differentiate again.
y′′ = 24x + 4
SECOND DERIVATIVE y′′ = 24x + 4
DIFFERENTIATION · 10

Applications of differentiation

Differentiation is useful for studying changing quantities in mathematics, physics, economics, engineering, and other fields.

1. Slope of a curve

The derivative gives the slope of the tangent line at a particular point.

2. Velocity

Definition

Velocity is the rate of change of position with respect to time.

Velocity = derivative of position

3. Acceleration

Definition

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time.

Acceleration = second derivative of position

4. Maximum and minimum values

Derivatives help us locate stationary points. At a stationary point, the first derivative is zero.

y′ = 0

A stationary point may be a maximum, a minimum, or another type of stationary point. Further analysis is needed to identify it.

Example 9 STATIONARY POINT
Question:

Find the stationary point of:

y = x² − 6x + 5
1
Differentiate.
y′ = 2x − 6
2
Set the derivative equal to zero.
2x − 6 = 0
3
Solve for x.
2x = 6
x = 3
4
Find the corresponding y value.
y = 3² − 6(3) + 5
y = 9 − 18 + 5
y = −4
STATIONARY POINT (3, −4)
PART II · INTEGRATION · 01

Introduction to integration

Definition

Integration is a mathematical process used to find accumulated quantities and antiderivatives.

Integration is closely related to differentiation. Differentiation finds a rate of change, while integration can recover the original function from its derivative, up to an additive constant.

Differentiation → Rate of change
Integration → Accumulation

The integration symbol

∫

The symbol ∫ is called the integral sign. It tells us that an integration operation is being performed.

INTEGRATION · 02

Antiderivatives

Definition

An antiderivative of a function is a function whose derivative equals the original function.

Consider the function:

y = x³

Its derivative is:

y′ = 3x²

Therefore, x³ is an antiderivative of 3x².

Antiderivatives can differ by a constant. Adding or subtracting a constant moves a graph vertically without changing its derivative.
Important observation

The functions x³, x³ + 2, and x³ − 2 all have the same derivative: 3x².

INTEGRATION · 03

Indefinite integrals

Definition

An indefinite integral represents a family of antiderivatives of a function.

∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C

Here:

  • ∫: integration symbol.
  • f(x): the expression being integrated.
  • dx: indicates integration with respect to x.
  • F(x): an antiderivative.
  • C: the constant of integration.

Basic example

∫ 2x dx = x² + C

Why? Because differentiating x² + C gives 2x.

INTEGRATION · 04

Power rule for integration

Definition

The power rule allows us to integrate a variable raised to a power.

∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹ / (n + 1) + C Valid when n is not −1.

Understanding the rule

There are two main operations:

  1. Add one to the original power.
  2. Divide by the new power.

For example:

∫ x² dx

Add one to the power:

2 + 1 = 3

Divide by the new power:

x³ / 3 + C
Example 10 POWER RULE
Question:
∫ 5x⁴ dx
1
Identify the coefficient and power.

Coefficient = 5. Power = 4.

2
Add one to the power.
4 + 1 = 5
3
Divide by the new power.
5x⁵ / 5
4
Simplify and add the constant.
x⁵ + C
FINAL ANSWER ∫ 5x⁴ dx = x⁵ + C
INTEGRATION · 05

Integration rules

1. Constant rule

∫ a dx = ax + C

When integrating a constant, multiply it by the variable and add the constant of integration.

Example 11 CONSTANT RULE
∫ 7 dx
1
Identify the constant.

The constant is 7.

2
Multiply by x.
7x
3
Add the integration constant.
7x + C
FINAL ANSWER 7x + C

2. Sum and difference rules

Sum rule

∫ (u + v) dx

Integrate both terms and add the results.

Difference rule

∫ (u − v) dx

Integrate both terms and subtract the results.

3. Constant multiple rule

∫ au dx = a ∫ u dx

A constant multiplier can remain outside the integral while we integrate the expression.

Example 12 MULTIPLE RULES
Question:
∫ (6x² + 4x + 8) dx
1
Separate the terms.
∫ 6x² dx + ∫ 4x dx + ∫ 8 dx
2
Integrate the first term.

Add one to the power and divide by 3.

6x³ / 3 = 2x³
3
Integrate the second term.
4x² / 2 = 2x²
4
Integrate the constant.
8x
5
Combine all terms.
2x³ + 2x² + 8x + C
FINAL ANSWER 2x³ + 2x² + 8x + C
INTEGRATION · 06

Constant of integration

Definition

The constant of integration, represented by C, accounts for all possible constant terms in an antiderivative.

Why do we need C?

Consider these three functions:

y = x² + 2
y = x² + 5
y = x² − 3

Their derivatives are all equal to 2x. This happens because the derivative of a constant is zero.

∫ 2x dx = x² + C
Remember

Do not forget + C when writing the general answer to an indefinite integral.

Finding a particular constant

Sometimes an additional condition gives us enough information to find C.

Example 13 FINDING C
Question:

Find the antiderivative of 2x if the resulting function has value 5 when x = 1.

1
Integrate.
y = x² + C
2
Use the given condition.

When x = 1, y = 5.

5 = 1² + C
3
Solve for C.
5 = 1 + C
C = 4
PARTICULAR ANTIDERIVATIVE y = x² + 4
INTEGRATION · 07

Definite integrals

Definition

A definite integral calculates the accumulated signed area over a specified interval.

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)

Here:

  • a: lower limit of integration.
  • b: upper limit of integration.
  • F: an antiderivative of the integrand.

Worked example

Example 14 DEFINITE INTEGRAL
Question:
∫₀² 2x dx
1
Find the antiderivative.
F(x) = x²
2
Substitute the upper limit.
F(2) = 2² = 4
3
Substitute the lower limit.
F(0) = 0² = 0
4
Subtract the lower result from the upper result.
4 − 0 = 4
FINAL ANSWER 4
Important distinction

Unlike an indefinite integral, a definite integral produces a numerical value. It does not include + C in its final evaluation.

INTEGRATION · 08

Area under a curve

Integration can be used to calculate the accumulated area between a curve and the horizontal axis over an interval.

The shaded region represents the area between the curve and the horizontal axis.
Area = ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx

When the curve is above the horizontal axis, the definite integral gives a positive value. When it is below the axis, its contribution is negative.

Important

A definite integral gives signed area. To find total geometric area when parts of the curve lie below the axis, integrate the absolute value of the function over each part separately.

INTEGRATION · 09

Relationship between differentiation and integration

Differentiation and integration are closely connected mathematical operations.

Integration reverses differentiation, up to the constant that differentiation removes.

Example: Differentiation followed by integration

Example 15 CONNECTING BOTH
Start with:
y = 3x² + 5x + 7
1
Differentiate.
y′ = 6x + 5
2
Integrate the derivative.
∫ (6x + 5) dx
3
Integrate each term.
= 6(x²/2) + 5x + C
4
Simplify.
= 3x² + 5x + C
FINAL RESULT 3x² + 5x + C

The constant term 7 was lost when we differentiated. Integration returns 3x² + 5x + C, and the original function is recovered only once C is determined from an additional condition, such as y = 7 at x = 0.

Formula summary

Use this section to review the principal algebraic differentiation and integration rules.

Differentiation formulas

RuleFormula
Constantd/dx(c) = 0
Powerd/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹
Sum(u + v)′ = u′ + v′
Difference(u − v)′ = u′ − v′
Constant multiple(cu)′ = cu′

RuleFormula
Constant∫ a dx = ax + C
Power∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C

Sum∫(u + v)dx = ∫u dx + ∫v dx

Important exception

The integration power rule requires n ≠ −1. Do not apply it when the power is −1; ∫ x⁻¹ dx = ln|x| + C instead.

FINAL CHAPTER

Conclusion

Differentiation and integration provide two important ways to understand mathematical functions.

Differentiation

y → y′

Studies rates of change and slopes of curves.

Integration

y′ → y + C

Finds antiderivatives and accumulated quantities.

Final thought

Do not memorize a formula without understanding what it does. Identify the expression, select the appropriate rule, apply it carefully, and simplify your result.
